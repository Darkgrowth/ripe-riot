# Sunpatch first-zone finish

20 September 2026. This builds on the dock/shop, worksite, orchard/glove and
harvest-hero passes. The target is a cohesive first-zone demo that can be shown
to new players. Commercial appeal still needs external playtesting; this is
not a statement that the whole game is ready for sale.

## Environment and close-up consistency

All apple and orange trees now use three distinct pruned crown shapes, cooler
foliage, more substantial support limbs and readable fruiting spurs. The exact
attachment points, collider/height contracts, plant IDs and RNG consumption
remain unchanged. The 24 other plant variants retain their original geometry.
Former separate proof batches are consolidated into the shared species batches.

Meadow, upland and damp grove floors use quieter regional color variation,
with soft worn-earth footprints beneath the workplaces. Rock faces have broad
mineral bands. These edits change vertex color only, not terrain height, slope,
path weights or collision. New fern and grass colonies use an independent RNG
and stay away from the harvesting route and workplace footprints.

Cave Grove now has a connected, irregular stone arch behind its existing fruit
plants, cool undergrowth and a readable harvesting warning. Its rendering and
physics use the same closed mesh. The arch has 78 vertices and 152 triangles.
Offline inspection found no fruit or plant-collider overlap; 333 player-capsule
samples across nine lines through the opening were clear. This static clearance
check alone does not prove movement. A subsequent actual W-key walk covered
15.28 m through the opening and exited grounded. See `qa/grove-audit.md` and
`qa/manual-walk.json` for the separate static and movement evidence.

Hill Farm and Palm Beach have restrained wear, empty tray storage, hanging
rope/equipment, a folded cloth and net floats. Their 42 existing colliders stay
exactly unchanged. Anchor crags gain broader strata with identical vertices and
collision arrays. The King Melon has broad wandering stripes, a recessed crown,
calyx, bent stem and blossom scar inside its original visual envelope. Its
position, radius, mass, vine anchors and existing material remain unchanged.

## Actual progression bug resolved

The intermittent vinebomb test was hiding two concrete issues. A waterfall
rock intersected an attached vinebomb, and its seeded launch pointed into the
steep terrain. Moving that single rock and its collider 3 m west clears the
node. Elastic fruit now reverse only an inward horizontal launch direction;
node location, tension, vertical component and RNG draws remain unchanged.

The old test treated any despawn as successful flight off the island, although
bursting and sinking also remove fruit. It now retains the fruit record and
asserts its actual measured travel. `vinebomb-check.mjs` checks the real
populated Rapier world: all seven nodes clear, 14 flights after fresh startup
and body churn pass. The waterfall fruit travels 10.366 m before sinking in
both runs; the other sites travel 16.15–32.47 m. This corrects the earlier
progression-only false positive documented in prior delivery notes.

## Cost

Apple trees are 1,540 triangles each, orange trees 1,160. The King Melon grows
from 2,160 to 4,346 triangles; workplace detail adds 996 triangles. Those
landmark changes add no materials or draw calls. The grove arch joins the
existing prop batch; its warning adds one textured sign mesh.

The original 1280×720 arrival baseline used 120 draw calls / 816,246 rendered
triangles. Its 45-frame software-renderer sample measured 384.5 ms median and
414.5 ms p95. This was ANGLE SwiftShader, not the machine's RTX 4070 Ti, so it
must not be quoted as hardware game performance. A fresh final arrival frame
uses 115 calls / 885,562 triangles, approximately 8.5% more rendered triangles
and five fewer calls. Textures increase from 16 to 17.

Full Chromium was separately verified to use the NVIDIA RTX 4070 Ti through
ANGLE D3D11. Three 600-frame samples at DPR 1 covered 1920×1080 arrival,
3434×1270 arrival and 1920×1080 orchard with 48 extra loose apples. Mean frame
times were 5.56–6.93 ms; p95 was 7.8–13.7 ms, with no frames above 33.34 ms.
These are short warmed samples, not a long-session or lower-end hardware
guarantee. Resolution comparisons are not meaningful across their varying
warm-up/OS conditions. Raw data: `qa/perf-hardware-final.json`.

## Acceptance results

The complete browser gameplay suite passed **10/10 scenarios, 323 checks**:
backcountry 40, carry 41, harvest loop 26, legendary 51, movement 17,
fruit physics 26, progression 32, ropes 18, startup 35 and tools 37.
The strengthened vinebomb check measured 10.37 m of real travel in the full
sequence, explicitly recording sinking rather than inferring success from
despawn. Raw results: `qa/gameplay.log`.

Local two-client multiplayer passed **127/127 checks**, including shared ropes
and disconnect/rejoin, with no reported console errors. This is BroadcastChannel
on one machine; real Internet multiplayer remains outside this pass.

The scripted fresh-save pacing run reached first sale at 1:18, net purchase at
2:07, shaker at 3:18, rope gun at 6:31, King Melon completion at 7:33 and Gale
Grove unlock at 7:37. It sold 36 fruit across three harvest runs. Its clock is
simulated game time; travel is teleported and charged by distance, and legendary
recovery has a scripted placement fallback. This establishes the progression
chain, not a human completion-time estimate. Raw output: `qa/playthrough.log`.

## Interface and direct play

The hotbar and shop share authored SVG tool silhouettes instead of platform
emoji. Locked shop entries remain readable. A compact click-to-play prompt
shows movement, look, picking and tool keys; after play it says click to return.
It hides while pointer-locked, shopping or reading the fruit book.

Actual keyboard/mouse checks covered dock walking, the grove arch, normal fruit
picking/stowing/dropping, E to open the shop, Escape to close it and pointer-lock
entry/return. The eight shop cards rendered their icons; no page errors were
recorded. Evidence: `qa/manual-walk.json`, `qa/ui-input.json`, `qa/normal-pick.json`,
`qa/entry-hint.png` and `qa/shop.png`.

The comparison gallery was checked at desktop and 390 px mobile widths,
has no horizontal overflow, and its sliders work. It is presentation evidence;
the game itself remains a desktop keyboard/mouse experience.

## Final hardware regression

The first hardware rerun exposed two real carry presentation failures hidden by
the slower software renderer. Tool/fruit proxy visibility waited for a real-time
stow ramp, briefly showing both hands/tool and carried fruit, or retaining a
dropped fruit proxy. Visibility now follows ownership immediately; pose animation
is retained. New assertions check the very next rendered frame, rather than
adding a longer wait. The targeted carry scenario passes **43/43** checks and
the final hardware suite passes **10/10 scenarios, 325 checks**. Raw initial
failure is retained in `qa/gameplay-hardware.log`; final results are in
`qa/carry-hardware-final.log` and `qa/gameplay-hardware-final.log`.

Startup/camera framing checks pass across five additional aspect ratios, and
the production build passes. The remaining build notice is the existing
approximately 2.93 MB JavaScript chunk. Startup's two missing-resource messages
were traced to `/favicon.ico`; an authored melon SVG favicon now resolves that
request path through the document's icon link. Final fresh boot and pointer-lock
check recorded no console/page/resource errors (`qa/final-console.json`).

The eight destination views, grove opening, cannon and two unobstructed beach
views were visually inspected. No new floating props, missing faces or clutter
blocking routes was found. The sparse hillsides and simplified crags remain
part of this low-poly presentation; this pass is not photoreal environment art.

Assessment: Sunpatch is a cohesive, polished playable demo ready for outside
first-session playtests. Art polish and an intact progression chain do not
establish commercial demand. First-time navigation, enjoyment, replay interest
and willingness to pay still need observation with new players. No claim of
retail readiness or projected sales is made here.
